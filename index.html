About

HEADLESS

A stacking game about faceless profiles. Legs, torso, a face-that-isn't-one: build the body the grid asks for, before everyone goes offline.

The app is already a game. This one just admits it.

Bodies by

Thank you to the queer artists who lent their bodies of work to this game. Every credited tile carries their name. Go follow them, buy their work, book them.

Why this game

HEADLESS is the playable half of a research project. The game is a way in: a reason to think, talk and write about the themes it plays with, from desire turned into a metric to the intimacy and agency we are handing over to machines. A longer text on digital intimacy is in progress.

Headless Agency

Every dating app has one: the torso with no head. A body on offer, a face held back. HEADLESS turns it into a game piece. Heads, torsos, legs and feet fall into the grid; you stack them into bodies, chase combos, collect the little hit of a reward.

Sound familiar? It should. The apps were games long before this one. Swipes, taps, boosts, paywalls, even ads for other games: all built to keep you on the grid, not to send you off it.

In tech, headless means a system that runs without its human interface: a browser with no window, a backend with no face. That is the bigger story. First we handed our attention to the feed. Now we are handing our intimacy to chatbots. Next, with AI agents, our agency. Each surrender makes the next one easier, and the head being removed is ours.

This is not a joke at the expense of the people on the apps. It is a joke at the expense of the machine. The bodies here come from queer artists, cropped with consent and credited above, and they point the grid towards everyone the filters skip.

A game cannot give us our heads back. It can, for a few minutes, make the machinery visible. It is a reminder that desire was never meant to be a score, and that the choice to stay, to leave or to look up is still ours to make.
